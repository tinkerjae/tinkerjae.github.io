Talking about the subconscious

(March 14, 2026)

So I’ve got an interesting topic this week.

Sometimes I get a kind of, theme? No actually, it’s more like a common element that keeps popping up. A theme is more like the “moral of the story” that things are revolving around. Rahhh anyways. I wanted to talk about the subconscious because it’s been cropping up in my life a lot recently.

Here are some examples:

  1. I’ve been getting back into magick and a common element of magick is using your subconscious in your favor.
  2. I’ve been noticing a pattern of self-hatred and self-loathing. I saw a video today by Dr. K titled “Why Self-Pity Can Feel So Good” that talks about the need for the subconscious to keep it’s mental model of the world consistent. Consistency trumps feeling good.
  3. I’ve been noticing that my cognitive, emotional, and other such “front of the mind” coping mechanisms failing or being inadequate as of recent. I’ve been trying to study and I tend to procrastinate or ruminate (see last weeks post). This is odd as I’m usually more on the ball, I think.

That being said, I’d like to write a bit on this to clear my head and maybe help my understanding of my situation. I’ve been trying to make changes in my life to help my study habits. I’ve had a bit of a break period recently and I’ve been trying to catch up on school and my hobbies. So far it’s not been the best. With school it feels like I’m pulling teeth to get anything done. As of now I’m assuming that’s because my strategy of school is intensity over consistency, not great but I’m trying to establish a routine of studying after work. It’s not perfect but since I work in the early mornings (5am is when I get up), studying in the mornings is probably more trouble than it’s worth.

Oh some good news, I got caught up in the course that’s been troubling me, Calculus 3. I’ve scored pretty low (50s and 40s) on the last 2 exams, but I’m hoping I can still bring it back. If not, it is what it is, I’ll try again next semester. I don’t want to drop the course, but I’ve got 3 weeks to decide if I need to. If I bomb the next exam or can’t recover to a B, I will unfortunately have to drop the course. It sucks too because I really like this class, I’ve just been in a rough period because I took too many classes this semester.

Anyways, around this whole thing with the subconcious. I’ve been trying to get myself studying by the old un-reliable method of being mean to myself in ways that are not helpful. That’s not something I’ve found a total solution for yet. Like I said I’ve tried the cognitive, emotional, and other such techniques. They all feel like band-aid fixes. They help a little in the moment but aren’t substantial enough. Also I can’t really go to therapy due to my busy lifestyle at the moment, so that’s out of the picture for now.

After watching a few videos on things involving the subconscious, magickal, therapeutic, or otherwise, I might’ve found a new lead. So, one thing I heard recently that stuck with me is that “You can’t argue or reason with your subconscious, you can only provide it different evidence.” And that makes a lot of sense. I mean you can’t tell someone to just love themselves. Even if they don’t consciously make an effort to be mean to themselves, and they can see the value of self-love, that’s not something in their direct control. It’s the subconscious mental model from lived experience that tells them “you are unlovable” or other things of that nature. So if my problem of self loathing, procrastination, and generally just being mean to myself is out of my direct cognitive or emotional control, then it’s probably under the control of my subconscious.

Okay… then what do we do? I reckon that if I can find an outline of what my mental model is like around the topic of self loathing and stuff, I can slowly but surely convince myself through real world experience of a different mental model.

So if the mental model I have causes me to respond with procrastination, rumination, self-loathing, and other stuff like that, then what is triggering that response? My first hypothesis is going to be something like:

Want to do something —> Compare where you’re at to where you want to be.
If Bad: Make yourself feel bad to get yourself working harder or to quit so you don’t have to do stuff that makes you feel bad anymore.
If Good: Move on to next thing.

Man, that’s rough lol. So if our mental model is causing us to do a comparison and either feel bad or move on, then there’s no wonder why this cycle is so vicious. We have our inputs and outputs tho, we don’t neccesarily need the internal logic of this system but a rough guess would be nice.

Hmm…. So my subconscious has a behavior^ that probably comes from some belief. What is that belief? ‘Something is only worth doing if we’re good at it’ Maybe not that. ‘If you’re bad at something you care about, you should feel bad about that.’ Okay well obviously that’s not true, but that seems pretty accurate. Well, why is this belief there? It’s probably there to save me from some perceived threat. Maybe it’s the threat of hard things being stressful. Probably but that’s likely not it. I think it’s a mixture of saving me from wasting my time with ‘useless’ effort, and to motivate me to do better. It’s trying to get me away from the bad feeling by either working hard or quitting. Well even when I work hard the feeling is still there, usually it actually gets worse. I’m going to assume this is a faulty/unintended response.

So my subconscious says “If you’re bad at something you care about, you should feel bad about that,” likely to get me to work harder or to quit and save my energy. Well, what’s another belief that doesn’t make me feel bad but still gets me to work hard when I’m bad at something? “If you’re bad you need to work harder.” Well that’s not gonna work, besides I already feel that way usually. “If you fail, try something different.” I think that’s already there too, it’s also not quite what I’m looking for. One belief that I don’t see is that “hard work always pays off,” as I’m very lazy if I let myself be.

I think my brain has a like, messed up relationship between what I enjoy and how I do at said thing. What can I do to prove to myself something to replace that previous belief. The problem is mainly the hatred, procrastination, self loathing, all that is really bad for problem solving and my health. What am I afraid of believing? I think that’s a good place to go. “If you work hard and are patient, you will get what you are looking for.” You see that scares me.

I’m someone who believes very much so in materialism, peoples circumstances can determine a lot about their life. But in recent months I’ve been trying to meld the ideas of perennialism and spirtual things with materialist conceptions of the world. I’m someone who thinks for example, if God is good, then God will not smite you or send you to hell for believing in a different God. I also think something like, enlightment, is achievable by anyone. Not easily, and most will not achieve it in their current lifetime, but I think it is possible. Because, if I believe that some are not meant to reach peace and happiness, then that’s a level of cosmic injustice I couldn’t bear. I know there’s evil terrible things that happen out in the world, but I can’t bear the thought that there is genuinely no hope for some people.

I bring this up, because of the idealist and materialist tensions that in part cause me to fair that way of thinking. “If you work hard and are patient, you will be find what you’re looking for,” feels like it’s an indictment of people who are unhappy due to their material conditions. If you’re born with 1 arm and no legs in a poor country with little oppurtunity, does this imply that you ought to just work harder? How the hell are you supposed to pull yourself up by bootstraps that you can’t even afford nor physically reach? I know that spiritual peace and good material conditions are mutually dependent, but I’m worried if I take on that belief, I’ll start to look down on others who ‘haven’t worked hard enough’ and such. That also means, that I could have everything I want but I’m just not good enough.

So I’m gonna guess that’s not a good replacement belief, but I think I have a new idea. Something like ‘Better is possible.’ A sort of hope. But this doesn’t guide me at all how I am supposed to get to better. And overtime as I fail and the light starts to dim, that belief will become twisted as it has been up until now. There’s one belief I already have that I could try leaning into more. Something like ‘There’s always another option,’ or ‘there’s always a solution.’

My head is starting to hurt from all this, I’ll stop for now lol.

Wait before I go, maybe I can prove to my subconcious by repeated exposure that hard work ain’t so bad, and that progress is possible.

I’ll settle on that, progress is possible.
