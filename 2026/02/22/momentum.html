Keeping your momentum is important!

(February 22, 2026)

This week has been interesting. I mostly want to talk about today though.

Today I studied a whole bunch on Calculus 3 with varying results. I’m a bit behind in this class as of right now. It’s not too bad so far, just things have been a little hard to keep up with due to my life being super busy. I got up and started studying around 9am. My original plan was to study from around 9am - 6pm. That would be a sum total of 9 hours if I was just studying. With breaks I thought probably more like 5-7 hours. Okay cool, well then I got started and everything went obviously according to plan.

At 11am I got distracted, I think I was doing a mixture of talking to my friends on the phone, family in the house, and trying to make breakfast. By about 2pm I finally got back on the horse again. At around 3pm again I felt stuck. Something was just not working. I took another break from 3pm-4pm.

At 5:30pm I began studying again, again 100% on schedule. At 6pm I realized it STILL wasn’t working. So I went and called a “friend” (you know who you are) and decided to give up on a problem that was giving me some trouble. By about 6:30pm I seemed to be getting somewhere. I thought, “I’ll just study till 7pm.” By 8pm I had to force myself to stop because I was doing so well.

So the main takeaways I got from this, other than I am very good at staying on schedule, is that I need to keep my momentum.

Oxford Dictionary defines momentum, probably, but I am not going to use their definition because that’s unhelpful. I’m defining momentum as the phenomenon where you’re doing something and it takes more effort to switch between things than it is to keep doing the same thing. You can call this inertia, resistance, habits, whatever, but I’ll be calling it momentum. It seems that at around 11am today I lost my momentum, attempted to restart my study session at 2pm and unfortunately ran into a huge momentum killer, to the point where I only really got it back at around 6pm.

The momentum killer, I suspect, was a particularly difficult homework problem about finding the vector parametrization of the intersection of 2 surfaces. I wrote it down somewhere but I don’t want to find it. I believe the problem killed my momentum not only because of the logic I’m proposing but also because this is a repeated phenomena. I have a tendency to stick it out on difficult homework problems, which can be helpful, but it can also be a hinderance. I’ve noticed myself stop my sessions frustrated most often because I kind of have a “thug it out” sort of mindset about these things. I really would like to get better at solving this problem I run into so often.

Anyways, I’m spending all these silly keystrokes to say that, you gotta conserve your momentum. I lost roughly 5 hours of an 11 hour total study session, and while that 11 hours isn’t healthy, it could’ve been much better if I had conserved my momentum. For future reference I’m gonna try and shelve super difficult problems whenever I hit them, and if I hit a few (say 3-5 ish ) in a row, I’ll slow down, take a break, come back and switch from the quick-minded mode I usually operate in to a more slow-and-steady approach that it takes to solve these difficult problems.

Hey that’s a good way of thinking of it. Speed vs Power. The difficult ones require much more power, and will require less over time which means more speed to keep me interested. Same thing with the easier problems. It might be that switching modes is what’s causing the momentum loss.

But yeah, I’ll shelve difficult problems to keep my momentum, and try to take it slower when solving those more difficult ones as to not burn myself out.
